# qrwty: commercial decision and Jeeves direction

Reviewed 31 October 2026. Synthesis of the three report sets, selected supporting research, current vendor pages, and Jeeves's current design and plan. No customer interviews or purchase tests were performed.

**My conclusion:** qrwty merits a small commercial test and is a useful reference product for Jeeves. The reports establish neither a profitable business nor a low ceiling on its eventual revenue. Build the reusable architecture to support a serious QR SaaS; make commercial commitments according to actual customer purchases.

These reports build on one another. Their agreement is not three independent confirmations of the market.

**What the internet check changes**

- QR.io advertises **$39.99/month or $350/year**, including bulk creation, API access, five team members, analytics and landing pages. A credible clone needs a complete customer workflow, beyond generating attractive codes. These are published offers, not evidence of QR.io's underlying architecture. [QR.io pricing](https://qr.io/pricing)
- Hovercode includes a custom domain at **$12/month** and team/bulk/API features at **$39/month**. Dub Links lists **three custom domains and QR codes on its free tier**, subject to usage limits. Cheap domains and an ownership slogan are therefore weak differentiation. [Hovercode](https://hovercode.com/pricing/), [Dub Links](https://dub.co/pricing/links)
- QuickCode's general page advertises **$10k–$150k+/year** enterprise delivery, while its enterprise page lists **$5k/$10k/$20k one-time packages**. This supports investigating private delivery; it does not support forecasting recurring contracts at those amounts. [General pricing](https://quickcode.digital/pricing), [enterprise packages](https://quickcode.digital/enterprise)

**The commercial choice**

Test hosted qrwty with print shops or small agencies that repeatedly manage client print. Sell a concrete job: create and update client links, report their use, and hand them over without reprinting. Demonstrate migration of the exact customer-owned hostname and path. Export cannot rescue a printed hostname owned by another vendor without that vendor's cooperation.

Use **$49/month as a test price**, not a researched optimum. Seek three unrelated paying businesses, continued use through another billing period, and manageable sales/support time. That earns a larger experiment. Private delivery becomes a separate offer when a named buyer funds a scoped pilot. Keep hardware, airgaps and speculative industry niches out of the initial commercial effort. Open-source licensing remains a product decision, not a substitute for distribution.

Distinguish **enterprise-capable software** from **an enterprise sales business**. Jeeves can help supply the former. Procurement, customer support, trust and distribution still require an operating business. Neither a cheap software factory nor a pessimistic revenue simulation settles that question.
