# Location-based games and QR scavenger-hunt products: business models and viability (2024-2026)

Note: research done with search-result summaries (WebSearch); most pages were not fetched in full. Figures marked (vendor) are vendor claims; (3rd) are third-party aggregators of uncertain age. About 14 tool calls.

## What do existing QR/location-hunt products charge, and to whom do they sell?

### Takeaway
Pricing is per event (GooseChase, Scavify, Loquiz) or per licence/year (Actionbound, Geocaching/Munzee subscriptions). Event pricing runs roughly $99-$750 self-serve to $1,000-$5,000 for managed corporate events. Buyers are corporate team-building, education and tourism/destination bodies. Retail/BID buyers are not evidenced in the results.

### Cited Findings
- GooseChase (3rd, figures inconsistent across listings): single-event plans of $99 (10 participants), $299 (40), $499 (100), $749 (175); a $9,999 department plan; educator plan around $49/user/year; K-12 around $99/year. Free tier limits differ by source. — [Software listing summary](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/goosechase/pricing); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/goosechase/pricing); [GooseChase pricing page](https://www.goosechase.com/pricing)
- Scavify: official page publishes no prices ("Get Pricing"). Third parties list a $995 turn-key and $1,995 full-service package (labelled seasonal examples) and ~$1,080/event starting, ~$1,300 for 100 attendees, $3,500-$5,000 for 500 (3rd estimates). Scavify's own blog (vendor, comparative) cites $649 for a 20-participant event versus GooseChase. — [Fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/scavify); [Eventfolio](https://eventfolio.pages.dev/tools/scavify); [Scavify blog](https://www.scavify.com/blog/scavify-vs-goosechase-app-review-features-pricing-top-alternatives); [Scavify pricing](https://scavify.com/team)
- Loquiz: Skift review (undated, possibly old) says EUR 50 per team, falling to EUR 25 (40 teams), EUR 15 (100 teams), EUR 10 (200 teams); no monthly fee. — [Skift Meetings](https://meetings.skift.com/?p=11298)
- Actionbound: licence tiers PRO (business; price depends on number of players), EDU and personal; sales contact for GPS events, white label. Schools: about 45 EUR/yr per teacher, about 380 EUR/yr per school (secondary source, undated); 2021 concept PDF says 49 EUR for a class up to 1,500 EUR for libraries. Some German states license it free to schools. — [Friedrich Verlag](https://www.friedrich-verlag.de/friedrich-plus/sekundarstufe/geographie/methodik-didaktik/die-gegend-erkunden-mit-smartphone-oder-tablet-2221); [Actionbound concept PDF](https://ifak-kindermedien.de/wp-03776-content/uploads/2021/07/Actionbound_Konzept.pdf); [Actionbound business licence](https://en.actionbound.com/license/business/1); [Saxony-Anhalt PDF](https://www.bildung-lsa.de/files/26b3dd0b87455c198649e5723887d7cc/SPZ_Schulrallye_mit_Actionbound.pdf)
- Geocaching (Groundspeak) Premium for new subscribers: $39.99/yr or $6.99/mo since 6 June 2023. — [Geocaching blog](https://www.geocaching.com/blog/?p=115116)
- Munzee premium: older figures about $29.99/yr or $2.99/mo (2018-era blog posts); no current price found. Eventzee (Munzee's event product) offered again as a $50 add-on per a 2026 Munzee blog post (unconfirmed on a pricing page); an older tutorial says it was unavailable. Munzee also sells "event packages" via the Freeze Tag store. — [Munzee blog 2026 events](https://munzeeblog.com/?p=46071); [Freeze Tag store event package](https://store.freezetag.com/products/event-package-1); [Munzee premium update](https://munzeeblog.com/an-update-to-premium-membership-benefits/)
- Seeker (trail-passport vendor) and Scavify sell tourism/destination hunts; the Scottsdale 75th anniversary hunt has 87 stops (vendor write-up). — [Seeker](https://seeker.io/blog/qr-codes-for-destination-marketing); [Scavify QR hunt](https://scavify.com/qr-code-scavenger-hunt)

### Inferences
- Self-serve per-event willingness to pay clusters at about $100-$750; managed corporate events reach $1,000-$5,000. A per-code print-and-place product for a small organiser would likely need to sit at or below the GooseChase tier or be subscription-based.
- Existing products are app/game-centric (photo/GPS missions), not physical-QR-first, which leaves a niche for printable codes, but the established price points are modest.

### Gaps
- Current official GooseChase, Actionbound, Loquiz pricing and any Let's Roam / CityHunt pricing: no reliable current figures retrieved.
- Customer mix by revenue (education vs corporate vs tourism) not found.

## What happened to Munzee as a QR-first game business?

### Takeaway
Munzee merged into Freeze Tag (OTC: FRZT) in an all-stock deal in October 2017; it is a small business inside a company with about $2M total revenue. No current player count or Munzee-specific revenue was found.

### Cited Findings
- Freeze Tag and Munzee agreed an all-stock merger in 2017; the combined company kept the Freeze Tag name and FRZT ticker. At announcement Munzee had almost 5 million locations and "hundreds of thousands" of players. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/press-release/freeze-tag-and-munzee-announce-merger-agreement-otc-pink-frzt-2228286.htm); [Munzee blog](https://munzeeblog.com/munzee-and-freeze-tag-merger/)
- Freeze Tag total revenue (all apps): about $1.98M FY2024, $2.12M FY2023, about $1.74M TTM to Sept 2025 (PitchBook/Morningstar table). — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/51151-06)
- Munzee still runs events and sells physical QR items (the blog is active through 2025-2026). — [Munzee blog](https://munzeeblog.com/?p=46071)

### Inferences
- A decade of a QR-first, physical-placement game yielded a niche business of roughly $2M/yr (likely mostly Munzee) rather than a platform company; a strong signal about the ceiling of the consumer game model.

### Gaps
- Munzee-only revenue, active players, and Freeze Tag filings (10-K) not retrieved.
- Geocaches total count not retrieved (only "millions").

## What did sponsored locations earn in Pokémon Go, and why did other location games fail?

### Takeaway
Niantic's disclosed price signal is under $0.50 per daily unique visit; total sponsorship revenue was never disclosed. Pokémon Go earned about $7.9B gross lifetime by 2024 from player spending; clones (Wizards Unite) shut down on weak installs and revenue.

### Cited Findings
- Niantic cost-per-visit model: partners spend less than $0.50 per daily unique visit; about 500 million visits to sponsored locations had been reported; payments never disclosed. — [Siliconera](https://vip-develop.siliconera.com/niantic-shares-details-revenue-generated-sponsorship-partners-pokmon-go); [PocketGamer.biz on sponsored locations](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-to-add-sponsored-locations)
- Pokémon Go lifetime: over $6B (Sensor Tower, June 2022); $7.9B gross / $5.5B net (AppMagic, 2024, app stores only, excludes sponsorship); H1 2024 revenue $361.4M, down 7.1% yoy; 2020 peak about $1.3B. — [GameWorldObserver](https://gameworldobserver.com/2022/06/06/pokemon-go-surpasses-6-billion-in-lifetime-revenue-generating-1-billion-per-year-on-average); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-go-catches-nearly-8-billion-in-eight-years)
- Pokémon Go Fest host cities earned large sums in 2024 (headline only; figures not verified). — [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-go-fest-host-cities-earned-mind-blowing-amount-in-2024-3025880)
- Niantic sold its games business (Pokémon GO, Pikmin Bloom, Monster Hunter Now) to Scopely (Savvy Games Group / Saudi PIF) for $3.5B; reported closed 29 May 2025 (single Russian-language source); one report mentions an extra $350M cash contribution (conflicts with headline). — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/pokemon-go-dev-niantic-confirms-plan-to-sell-game-business-to-scopely-for-3-5-billion/); [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/143439/niantic-sells-pokemon-go-scopely); [WNHub](https://wnhub.io/ru/news/other/item-47944); [TechLeap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/niantic-sold-to-scopely-for-3-5b)
- Wizards Unite: shutdown announced Nov 2021, closed 31 Jan 2022; about 739k installs in first 10 months of 2021 (down 57% yoy, Sensor Tower via TechCrunch); Niantic's CEO reportedly called it "mediocre". Niantic gave no formal post-mortem. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2228050); [Games.SI](https://videogames.si.com/news/harry-potter-wizards-unite-shut-down-reason); [GameWorldObserver](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/11/03/niantic-shutting-down-harry-potter-wizards-unite-as-its-revenue-falls-short-compared-to-pokemon-go)
- Minecraft Earth: no post-mortem found.

### Inferences
- Even at the sponsored-location cost of up to $0.50/visit, the value rested on a huge audience; an organiser's own small-scale codes cannot rely on that traffic. The sponsorship model is a useful price anchor (cents per verified visit) for per-code visit pricing.

### Gaps
- Sponsor count, price per sponsored location, sponsorship revenue: not disclosed in anything found.
- Minecraft Earth reasons, and detailed causes of clone failures beyond weak installs/IP dependence.

## Retail/mall/BID and tourism case studies; engagement data (completion and scan rates)

### Takeaway
Only thin evidence: one mall campaign with participation numbers (2014) and vendor claims about dashboards. No BID case study and no independent completion or per-code scan-rate benchmarks were found.

### Cited Findings
- VivoCity (Singapore, 2014): QR-mascot campaign drew 8,618 scanners; 302 vouchers collected (about 3.5% of scanners). — [Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/video/case-study-how-vivocity-brought-qr-codes-to-life/363752)
- Mayfair Mall (Wisconsin): hidden QR codes with sweepstakes entries (1, 5, 10 entries for scans 1-3); no numbers. — [BizTimes](https://biztimes.com/bubbler-news-6)
- Tyler, TX parks QR hunt (12 clues); no numbers. — [KETK](https://digital-release.ketk.com/news/outdoors/tyler-parks-and-recreation-updates-their-scavenger-hunt-program/amp/)
- Vendors (Seeker, Scavify) say dashboards show completion and per-venue pull, and that hunts lift engagement; no quantified benchmarks (vendor). — [Seeker](https://seeker.io/blog/11-ways-to-boost-engagement-of-tourism-trails); [Scavify](https://scavify.com/qr-code-scavenger-hunt)
- A QR-linked mall display saw 43% of visitors access the QR code (not a hunt). — [Fujifilm](https://fujifilm.com/au/en/business/photofinishing/instax/instax-biz/case-studies/mjpm)

### Inferences
- Vendor emphasis on per-venue/completion dashboards suggests organisers (tourism, retail) value location-level analytics, but no direct evidence of demand or willingness to pay for per-code analytics was found; the mechanic is what is sold.

### Gaps
- Evidence that organisers want per-code placement analytics: none independent.
- BID/downtown case studies, scavenger-hunt completion rates, scans per placed code: not found.

## Legal and safety considerations

### Takeaway
Two main risks: municipal sticker/postering bans on public property and promotion law (lottery = prize + chance + consideration). Both are jurisdiction-specific and need local advice.

### Cited Findings
- NYC treats stickers/flyers on public property (poles, bins, lampposts) as unlawful; a recent directive covers affixing stickers on public or private property; a 2015 report cites $75 first-offence fine (possibly outdated). Other cities proposed $200-$1,000 per sticker per day; San Antonio considered treating stickers as graffiti. — [NYC BIC directive](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/bic/downloads/pdf/commission-directive-unlawful-placement-of-advertisements-on-property-12-17-25.pdf); [AMNY](https://www.amny.com/news/hes-stuck-on-removing-illegal-fliers-and-stickers); [Inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/aldermen-crack-down-on-sticker-mania-branding-it-guerrilla-marketing); [KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/2013/08/29/san-antonio-city-council-moves-to-treat-stickers-same-as-graffiti)
- Fake QR stickers on parking meters are a live fraud problem in 2025-2026, so cities actively remove unknown QR stickers and users are warned against scanning them (trust/safety headwind). — [NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/news/2025/06/09/nyc-parking-meter-scam-fake-qr-codes); [1News NZ](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/14/fake-qr-code-payment-stickers-found-on-christchurch-parking-meters/)
- Promotion law: lottery needs prize, chance and consideration; sweepstakes drop consideration (need free alternate method of entry, "no purchase necessary"); contests need genuine skill; some states (e.g. Florida, New York) restrict purchase requirements, and some require registration/bonding above prize thresholds (law-firm and vendor guides, not statutes). — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/files/Publication/bb8de679-e39c-4023-b100-944bb7c80747/Presentation/PublicationAttachment/b4d781c5-7bb4-423f-9d85-018c7817c9df/1320.pdf); [Gleam](https://gleam.io/guides/no-purchase-necessary); [Olshan](https://www.olshanlaw.com/f-sweepstakes-law-basics.html)

### Inferences
- Product implication: organiser-owned or permissioned placement (shops, malls, venues) is safe; public street furniture needs permits. A platform should ship permission prompts and sweepstakes-compliance templates.

### Gaps
- Trespass specifics and non-US (UK/EU) rules not researched; no safety-incident data for location games found.
