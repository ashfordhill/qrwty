## 1. Official current pricing: location-game / scavenger-hunt platforms

### Takeaway
GooseChase and Actionbound and Loquiz publish real prices (per-experience, per-player-licence, per-team respectively); Scavify and Let's Roam are quote-only. Geocaching Premium is $39.99/yr.

### Cited Findings
- GooseChase (fetched page): Free $0 (per Experience; up to 3 participants, one Experience at a time, up to 24 hours). Single Experience $99 USD one-time per Experience (up to 10 participants, one live Experience, 30 days to launch and run). Subscription, K-12 Educator, Non-profit and Custom are quote/not listed — [GooseChase pricing](https://www.goosechase.com/pricing)
- Actionbound Professional licence, billed per "Bound player" (each person playing a Bound): 50 players EUR 350; 100 EUR 600; 200 EUR 900; 500 EUR 1,500; Enterprise Flat (up to 2,500 players, one location) EUR 2,250. Licence term 1, 2 or 3 years (page gives no per-duration price). Add-ons: Multi-language Bounds PRO EUR 350; Bound Styler PRO EUR 550/yr. Test mode free — [Actionbound business licence](https://en.actionbound.com/license/business/1)
- Loquiz: 14-day free trial (1 open game). Monthly EUR 9 per team per month (1-100 teams/yr). Yearly EUR 600/yr, EUR 5 per team (101-1,000 teams/yr). Custom for 1,000+ teams. Edu package exists, no price listed. Prices exclude VAT. This supersedes the older Skift EUR 50/team figure — [Loquiz pricing](https://www.loquiz.com/pricing/)
- Scavify: no published prices; segment pages (Universities, Employers, Conferences, Tourist Destinations) say "Get Pricing"; free to create a first hunt; app free for participants; options DIY, Full Service, Ready-to-Go — [Scavify pricing page](https://www.scavify.com/pricing)
- Let's Roam: pricing page URL tried (letsroam.com/pricing) returned 404. Not fetched.
- Geocaching Premium: $39.99 for one year, $6.99/month (payments page, fetched; shows about $3.33/month equivalent) — [Geocaching payments](https://payments.geocaching.com/)
- Munzee Premium: only an undated Munzee blog price found ($29.99/yr, $2.99/mo); 2026 posts say monthly/yearly in-app and yearly online but give no price. Eventzee returning in 2026 as a $50 add-on per a December 2025 Munzee blog post (search snippet; page not opened) — [Munzee blog events 2026](https://munzeeblog.com/?p=46071)

### Inferences
- Units differ widely: per-experience (GooseChase $99), per-player licence (Actionbound about EUR 6-7 per player at 50, falling to about EUR 3 at 500), per-team (Loquiz EUR 5-9). Effective price at 500 players: Actionbound EUR 1,500 vs Loquiz about 100 teams x EUR 5 (team size unknown).
- GooseChase $99/10-participant entry and Actionbound EUR 350/50-player entry are the clearest anchors for a small-organiser willingness to pay.

### Gaps
- Let's Roam, Scavify numeric prices, Munzee Premium current price (no pricing page fetched).

## 2. Campaign tools and sign-placement tracking

### Takeaway
Ecanvasser and NationBuilder publish prices (per database size / monthly). Several small apps do track yard signs, and at least one (Flyra) ties a QR code to a placement pin. So "no sign-tracking product exists" in the earlier note is wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Ecanvasser plan cards: Core $99/mo (database 2,500 / 20,000 / 50,000 contacts); Pro $599/mo (250k / 500k / 1M); Enterprise custom, 12-month minimum. Unlimited users; priced on contacts or houses (whichever higher). Annual = "2 months free". Add-ons +$99/mo each: lead prospecting (US), real-time field tracking (Core), API, enhanced support. The page is internally inconsistent ("$234 billed monthly" label; calculator shows Core $299, Pro $1,299) — [Ecanvasser pricing](https://www.ecanvasser.com/pricing)
- NationBuilder: Starter $34/mo (up to 5,000 contacts); Pro $160/mo (up to 10,000 contacts); add-on packs $29-$89/mo; Enterprise custom. Monthly billing is 20% more than annual; billed on database size — [NationBuilder pricing](https://nationbuilder.com/pricing)
- Yard-sign trackers (search snippets, vendor/App Store listings; not individually opened): SignTrack app (team members only), ElectHelp (iPhone, free, tracks signs given to volunteers and retrieval), RouteReach (map-based assignment of yard signs/door hangers), Trailblz (lawn-sign request and install tracking in mobile canvassing app, KB 383), CampaignHQ (requests, deliveries, historical locations), Webelect "Sign Delivery Module" — e.g. [SignTrack](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/signtrack-app/id982585645), [Trailblz KB](https://www.trailblz.com/kb/images/kb0383/KB_383%20-%20Tracking%20Lawn%20Signs%20with%20the%20Mobile%20Canvassing%20App.pdf), [Webelect](https://v5.webelect.net/pub/docs/show/sign-delivery-module)
- Flyra: when a rep drops a "Yard Sign" pin, "that placement is recorded against a location", so later QR scans trace to a specific address; QR tracking "included with your plan". Page does not say whether location is GPS or manual, and gives no price — [Flyra help](https://helpdesk.flyra.io/articles/9963328-qr-code-tracking-for-signs-and-flyers)

### Inferences
- Placement-pin + QR attribution at placement time exists at least in Flyra; this partly contradicts the earlier "none found" conclusion in ooh_advertising_events.md. Competition for the concept is real, though small apps.

### Gaps
- Campaign Sidekick and NGP VAN/MiniVAN prices not fetched this pass (earlier note: third-party listings only). Not confirmed whether VAN publishes prices. Flyra pricing not found.

## 3. Scan-rate evidence for QR on print/OOH

### Takeaway
Still no independent OOH scan-per-impression study. Only vendor benchmarks, with scan rate defined as share of people who see a code and scan it; most are not tied to impressions.

### Cited Findings
- Definition used by vendors: scan rate = percentage of people who see a QR code and scan it; needs exposure counts, unlike scan count — via search summary of [QR-Code-Generator benchmark](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/qr-code-scan-rate-benchmark/) and [Linkbreakers](https://linkbreakers.com/help/article/qr-code-scan-rate-benchmarks-by-industry) (pages not opened)
- Linkbreakers (vendor): print ads in magazines/newspapers 1-4%; in-store signage 3-8%; packaging 5-15%; no outdoor figure — same Linkbreakers link
- OAAA/Morning Consult: 78% of OOH viewers took some action after seeing an ad (any action, not QR) — via a search summary of vendor content; primary OAAA report not fetched
- Probst 2012 undergraduate paper (uwlax.edu) returned 404; not fetched.

### Inferences
- Treat 1-4% print as the only vendor-stated anchor and label it unverified. Real poster scan rates are likely far lower per passerby, but no source states that.

### Gaps
- Scans per impression for posters/OOH; Vistar/Talon/Flowcode/Uniqode benchmark methodology not found.

## 4. Dynamic-QR vendor location features

### Takeaway
All three give scanner-side location (IP-based city level, optionally scanner GPS with opt-in). None found that records the poster's physical coordinates at creation.

### Cited Findings
- Bitly: location by country and city per link (Links page toggle, CSV export), dashboard widget "Clicks + scans by location", unidentifiable shown as "No city data"; API has QR scans-by-city and by-country endpoints. Plan gating of city data is claimed by a third party only — [Bitly support](https://support.bitly.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038058691)
- Flowcode: two levels, general location and advanced GPS-level; Growth and Enterprise plans see geolocation at two levels (help centre); "Advanced Geolocation Data Interstitial" asks scanners to click "Allow" before collecting precise location; marketing claims zip-code-level map — [Flowcode geo blog](https://www.flowcode.com/blog/what-is-geo-data-for-qr-codes-and-what-can-it-unlock-for-your-brand), [Flowcode help](https://intercom.help/flowcode/en/articles/7061686)
- QR TIGER: GPS tracking uses the scanner's device GPS; scanners are redirected to a permission page and must consent; also a geofence option restricting where a code can be scanned (that is a location check at scan, not placement capture). A listicle says location tracking needs the $49/yr Core plan (unverified) — [QR TIGER help](https://qrtiger.helpscoutdocs.com/article/135-set-up-gps-tracking-and-restrict-location), [QR TIGER GPS page](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/gps-qr-code)
- Hovercode notes that people scanning a poster probably will not share location and may drop off at the prompt — [Hovercode](https://help.hovercode.com/article/14-gps-tracking)

### Inferences
- Opt-in GPS prompts add friction to poster scans, so scan-side GPS is weak; placement-time capture (as in Flyra) avoids consent friction.

### Gaps
- Pages fetched only via search summaries; none opened in full.

## 5. Green, Krasno et al. 2016 lawn signs

### Takeaway
Four randomized field experiments, pooled result: signs increased advertised candidates' vote share, with some spillover to adjacent untreated precincts. The exact effect size could not be read from the paper.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Green, Krasno, Coppock, Farrer, Lenoir, Zingher (2016), Electoral Studies 41:143-150, DOI 10.1016/j.electstud.2015.12.002. Abstract: collaboration with a congressional candidate, a mayoral candidate, an independent-expenditure campaign against a gubernatorial candidate and a county commissioner candidate; signs planted in randomly selected voting precincts; pooled results suggest signs increased vote share; some evidence of spillover — [Coppock copy](https://alexandercoppock.com/green_etal_2016.html)
- Press (via search summary, not opened): Krasno quoted as 1.7 percentage-point gain; 376 precincts in New York, Pennsylvania, Virginia — [Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/?p=49072)
- Authors characterise the effect as small, on par with direct mail (search summary).

### Gaps
- Exact estimate, SE/CI and per-study precinct counts: PDF was compressed and unreadable by the fetch tool; the 1.7 point and 376 figures are second-hand.
