# QR codes and placement tracking on political signs, posters and canvassing literature (US, 2024-2026)

Note: research was limited (~13 tool calls). Search-tool summaries were used for several items; URLs are from search results, and some pages were not opened in full. Items marked [vendor] are vendor/self-reported. No source with scan-rate or conversion data for political QR codes was found.

## Do campaigns already put QR codes on signs, and is there scan-rate or conversion data?

### Takeaway
QR codes on political signs exist (advice blogs, vendor guides, isolated examples such as a Salina, KS QR-only sign), but the commentary is opinion and marketing. I found no published scan-rate or conversion data for yard signs. Nothing found about per-placement (per-sign) tracking.

### Cited Findings
- Campaigns & Elections article "Time for QR codes on campaign signs" (author Ben Donahower, a campaign operative who sells yard signs; dated Feb 15, 2012) says QR codes mostly add clutter, suit walkable/young/tech districts, should be tested first on flyers/mail with scan tracking, and that linking to a homepage "isn't effective". Its only stat ("one third of smartphone users have scanned QR codes") is unsourced and 14 years old. — [Campaigns & Elections](https://campaignsandelections.com/campaigntech/time-for-qr-codes-on-campaign-signs)
- Vendor/advice blogs recommend QR codes on signs, T-shirts, ads, for site visits, volunteer sign-ups, or "request a yard sign" [vendor]. — [Uniqode](https://www.uniqode.com/blog/trending-use-cases/qr-codes-for-election-campaigns); [The Campaign Workshop](https://thecampaignworkshop.com/blog/advocacy-campaigns/qr-codes); [708media](https://708media.com/qrcode/ways-to-use-qr-codes-in-a-political-campaign)
- Local news reported a Salina, Kansas candidate using a sign with only a scannable QR code (date not confirmed in my notes). — [Yahoo News/Salina](https://news.yahoo.com/reach-voters-one-candidate-salina-090126725.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall)
- A 2011 Toronto article noted QR codes appearing on election signs (shows the idea is old). — [BlogTO](https://media.blogto.com/tech/2011/04/qr_codes_make_an_appearance_on_election_signs)
- Yard-sign printers sell political signs at about $3-5 (small) to $4-8 (large) each in bulk; the page has no QR mention and no date [vendor]. — [Easy Signs](https://www.easysigns.com/news/details/how-much-do-political-yard-signs-cost)

### Inferences
- Static QR codes are commodity (free generators); the unproven part is unique-per-placement tracking, which no source showed anyone doing on signs.
- The only advice on measurement is "test on mailers first", which suggests literature (mail/door hangers) may be the easier test bed than signs.

### Gaps
- No scan-rate, conversion, or case-study data on political QR codes (searches returned vendor marketing only). No data on QR on door hangers or mailers specifically. Vistaprint, Signs.com, SpeedySigns QR offerings not checked.

## Campaign spending on signs and literature; number of campaigns per cycle

### Takeaway
No source found giving signs/literature as a share of budgets or the OpenSecrets/FEC category totals. Per-campaign sign orders range from tens (city council) to thousands (state legislature). Candidate counts are large: about 97,000 total candidates in 2024 per Ballotpedia, and over 500,000 local elected officials.

### Cited Findings
- Local printers quote $3-$15 per sign; a printer said a state rep candidate might need thousands of signs while a city council candidate might need 25; 2026 guide puts small batches at about $200-$1,000 and larger campaigns at $2,000-$6,000+. — [Hidden Costs Behind the Signs (Jan 9, 2026)](https://thevisualcommunicationguy.com/2026/01/09/the-hidden-costs-behind-the-signs-you-see-on-lawns/); [OCNJ Daily (Jan 16, 2026)](https://ocnjdaily.com/news/2026/jan/16/how-campaigns-approach-yard-sign-budgeting); [price guide 2026](https://new.risingsunartscentre.org/news/campaign-yard-sign-costs-price-guide-2026.html) (the last is a low-quality source)
- Washington PDC records show individual candidates' sign printing line items of about $767-$12,800 and literature printing about $145-$15,200 (single-campaign snapshots, race level unverified). — [WA PDC example](https://www.pdc.wa.gov/political-disclosure-reporting-data/browse-search-data/candidates/689531/expenditures)
- 2024 major-party candidates: 1,800 U.S. House (833 D / 967 R), 226 U.S. Senate, 9,685 state house; 16-35% fewer major-party congressional candidates than prior cycle. — [Ballotpedia News, Sep 16 2024](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2024/09/16/16-35-fewer-major-party-candidates-ran-for-u-s-congress-in-2024-than-in-the-previous-election-year/)
- Total 2024 candidates across all offices: 97,167 — [Ballotpedia Candidate Connection report 2024](https://ballotpedia.org/Ballotpedia's_2024_Candidate_Connection_report) (as relayed by search summary; not opened)
- More than 500,000 local elected officials in US; Ballotpedia covered 23,454 elections in H1 2025 and about 66,000 local offices since 2023. — [Ballotpedia News, Jul 10 2025](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/07/10/ballotpedia-has-covered-at-least-one-election-every-week-through-the-first-half-of-2025/)
- Federal 2024 total cost projected $15.9B (OpenSecrets projection, as relayed). Winning House campaign ~ $3.1M is a third-party (Invezz) extrapolation, not OpenSecrets. — [Invezz, Feb 2024](https://invezz.com/news/2024/02/22/cost-breakdown-of-us-elections/)

### Inferences
- Addressable base is mostly local and state-legislative: tens of thousands of candidates, with small budgets where sign spending is a large share; these are price-sensitive buyers.

### Gaps
- OpenSecrets/FEC disbursement category totals for "printing/signs/literature" not found. No share-of-budget figure. Number of state-legislative and local candidates beyond the above not found.

## Existing tools for tracking sign placement, literature drops, canvassing, and pricing

### Takeaway
Canvassing tools (NGP VAN/MiniVAN, Ecanvasser, NationBuilder, Campaign Sidekick) exist at roughly $34-$199/month listed prices; I found no product dedicated to sign-placement tracking or QR attribution (only a quick search; not exhaustive).

### Cited Findings
- Ecanvasser: Capterra lists Starter EUR 99/mo and Standard EUR 199/mo, Enterprise on request; another listing says from $99/mo (currency conflict). — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/156872/Ecanvasser/pricing/); [Softwarefinder](https://softwarefinder.com/sales-tools/ecanvasser) [third-party listings]
- NGP VAN: no official public price; GetApp lists 45 per user/month (unverified); MiniVAN reportedly bundled with VAN access via party agreements (a competitor blog claim, unverified). — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/ngp-van/pricing); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/government-social-services-software/a/ngp-van/alternatives); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/ecanvasser-pricing-reviews-pros-and-cons)
- Campaign Sidekick about $99/user/month per directories — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/government-social-services-software/a/campaignsidekick). NationBuilder from about $34/mo listed — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/130934/NationBuilder/pricing/). Civitech Campaign OS $50-250/mo (2022, dated) — [Higher Ground Labs](https://highergroundlabs.com/?p=1849)

### Inferences
- A hardware sticker printer would sit outside the monthly-SaaS price anchors of $34-$199; it would need to integrate with or beat spreadsheets (common tracking method, not sourced).

### Gaps
- Mobilize, i360, EveryAction pricing; any "sign tracking" apps; whether these tools have a sign-request/sign-location object. Not searched.

## Regulations: disclaimers, sign ordinances, privacy

### Takeaway
Yard signs need a "paid for by" disclaimer (the small-item exemption is narrow); removal deadlines range from 2 to 30 days after election by jurisdiction; no source found directly on QR scan tracking of voters by campaigns.

### Cited Findings
- FEC: yard signs are printed public communications requiring a disclaimer; exemption (11 CFR 110.11(f)(1)(i)) is for bumper stickers, pins, buttons, pens and similar small items. — [FEC, "Don't forget your disclaimers" 2026](https://www.fec.gov/updates/dont-forget-your-disclaimers-2026/); [FEC canvassing tip](https://www.fec.gov/updates/door-to-door-canvassing-tip20180709). Note: this is the federal rule; state disclaimer rules were not researched. A small QR sticker could plausibly fall under small items (inference).
- Removal deadlines: La Mesa CA 5 days; Omaha 7; Littleton CO, Santa Clarita, Panama City FL 10; Las Vegas/North Las Vegas, Selah WA 15; Montana 14; Nevada state highway 30 days. Some assign responsibility to the placer (Omaha). — [Fox5 Vegas, Nov 2024](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2024/11/18/when-do-political-signs-come-down-after-election-day/); [Littleton CO policy 2024](https://www.littletonco.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/2/city-clerk/elections/2024-election/political-sign-policy-rev-2024.pdf); [Explore Big Sky](https://explorebigsky.com/tuesday-is-deadline-to-remove-political-signs/19745)
- QR scan logging commonly records code ID, coordinates, timestamp and user agent; compliance vendors advise a privacy policy when tracking cookies/personal data are collected [vendor]. — [PageLoot](https://pageloot.com/blog/qr-code-privacy-laws-key-regulations/)
- A 2019 report said no federal law regulates political organizations' voter-data use; state voter-file rules vary and are seldom enforced. — [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/f/614048/a-new-bill-aims-to-protect-us-voters-from-the-next-cambridge-analytica/); [Wisconsin Law Review (Rubinstein)](https://wlr.law.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1263/2015/02/1-Rubinstein-Final-Online.pdf)
- Campaigns & Elections reported early signs that campaigns weren't ready for California's privacy law (details not read). — [C&E](https://campaignsandelections.com/campaigntech/early-signs-indicate-campaigns-aren-t-ready-for-california-s-digital-privacy-law/)

### Inferences
- Placement-level (not voter-level) tracking, where only aggregate scans per sign are counted, likely has low privacy risk; the risk arises if scans are tied to identifiable voters. Needs legal review.
- Removal deadlines create a real use case: a log of GPS-tagged placements helps crews retrieve signs before fines.

### Gaps
- State-level disclaimer rules, CCPA applicability to campaigns, and any QR-specific political legal guidance not found.

## Evidence on sign effectiveness and whether campaigns pay for attribution

### Takeaway
Best evidence: lawn signs raise vote share by roughly 1-1.7 points on average in four randomized experiments, with possible spillover to adjacent precincts. I found no evidence that campaigns pay for sign attribution.

### Cited Findings
- Green, Krasno, Coppock, Farrer, Lenoir, Zingher (2016), Electoral Studies 41:143-150: four randomized field experiments (NY, PA, VA; 376 precincts; congressional, mayoral, independent-expenditure gubernatorial, county commissioner); pooled effect positive; spillover to neighboring precincts. — [Yale ISPS](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps16-23); [Coppock page](https://alexandercoppock.com/green_etal_2016.html)
- Effect size reported as slightly over 1 percentage point (journalist summary) versus 1.7 points (Krasno quote) - conflicting; verify in paper. — [Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/?p=49072); [NHPR](https://www.nhpr.org/post/traditional-lawn-sign-may-still-matter-even-crowded-nh-primary)

### Inferences
- The experiments randomized at precinct level; a ~1 point effect suggests signs matter, but per-sign scan data is not what the research measured. Scans would measure engagement, not votes.

### Gaps
- No evidence that campaigns pay for or use sign-level attribution; no study linking QR scans to outcomes.

## Seasonality and analogous buyers

### Takeaway
Demand is cyclical and concentrated before elections; Ballotpedia shows elections nearly every week, with a broad local footprint. Advocacy buyers were not researched in depth.

### Cited Findings
- Ballotpedia covered at least one election every week in H1 2025 (47 election dates; 96% included a local office), implying off-year and odd-year local demand. — [Ballotpedia News, Jul 10 2025](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/07/10/ballotpedia-has-covered-at-least-one-election-every-week-through-the-first-half-of-2025/)
- Municipal elections occur in even and odd years (Ballotpedia municipal election pages 2024/2026). — [Ballotpedia municipal 2026](https://ballotpedia.org/United_States_municipal_elections,_2026)

### Inferences
- Primary-to-general placement window is likely a few months; the hardware would be mostly idle after Nov (unsourced inference). Rental or service model may fit better than purchase.

### Gaps
- No data on unions, ballot initiative campaigns, nonprofits, or sign-buying timing (not searched). Budget cycles and purchase timing unverified.
